Introduction

A brief note before we begin.

Dear Craig, Andrew, and Chris,

My name is Khubaib. I'm a consultant in the Actuarial team, one of this year's graduate representatives on the Grad & Vacationer Committee, and a co-founder of the Canberra Games Club. I'm putting this proposal forward in each of those capacities, because the idea sits at the intersection of all three.

I'm bringing it specifically to the three of you because it touches the portfolios you each hold: consulting culture and team engagement, the broader ACT office identity, and the graduate and vacationer experience. It is a small idea, with a deliberately modest ask, but one that addresses a recurring piece of feedback I have heard from the graduate cohort and the wider office over the past several months.

The remainder of this document outlines what is being proposed, why it is worth doing, and how we propose to take ownership of it. There are two distinct asks: Craig and Andrew's approval for the table to be used in Room 8.10 and alongside our Canberra office events, and a budget request to Chris through the Grad & Vacationer Committee for the initial outlay of ~$200–$300.

The Concept

One room. Two purposes.
No compromise to either.

Room 8.10 currently sits on Level 8 and is typically used for large internal team meetings. The proposal is to introduce a folding table tennis table that lives in the existing storage area when the room is in use as a meeting space, and folds out when it is not.

Functionally unchanged.

Room 8.10 continues to operate exactly as it does today, supporting the large internal team meetings it is currently used for. Bookings are unaffected. The room's appearance during a meeting is unchanged.

The folding table tennis table is stored vertically in the existing storage area when the room is booked. Setup and pack-down take under two minutes.

An informal, optional second life.

Outside of meeting bookings — and in particular during monthly Friday drinks, lunch breaks, or after hours — the room becomes available as a social space for those who wish to use it.

The room can stay closed in its usual configuration to contain the sound, or be opened up to flow into the surrounding floor during office drinks, depending on the occasion.

The proposal begins with a folding table tennis table only. We may come back and request a console upon a successful trial.

Rationale

Three reasons it earns its place.

Cultural, experiential, and practical — each benefit maps directly to a priority already championed within the office.

01

Culture & connection

Casual, low-pressure interactions across teams and seniorities build the relationships project work depends on. Culture is built in the moments between the work — this adds more of those, intentionally, without disrupting it. A natural complement to the monthly Friday drinks and a reason for people to linger a little longer.

02

Graduate & vacationer experience

A tangible signal that the office invests in the early-career experience. This idea has come directly from the graduate cohort, and acting on it builds genuine trust in the feedback loop — including for the incoming vacationers at the end of the year, who will arrive within the six-month trial window.

03

A reason to be in the office

In a hybrid environment, the office benefits from offering experiences the home setup cannot. A short physical break, a shared social moment, and a small but visible point of difference for Canberra — relevant to attendance, wellbeing, and how the office is perceived by graduates and visitors alike.

The Ask

A folding table tennis table for Room 8.10. That is the proposal.

Two asks: Craig and Andrew's approval for the table to live in Room 8.10, and a budget request to Chris through the Grad & Vacationer Committee for the initial outlay. The purchase itself is deliberately narrow — a single folding table from Kmart with basic equipment, on a six-month trial.

Accountability

Clear ownership, from day one.

A new shared resource only succeeds if someone is genuinely on the hook for it. The Canberra Games Club will organise its use, with support from the Grad & Vacationer Committee (CGVC) if needed.

Documented usage guidelines

Clear written rules covering when the room is bookable for social use versus meetings. Calendar bookings always take priority.

Six-month review

A formal review at the conclusion of the trial — covering usage, feedback, and any issues raised — reported back to the three of you before any next step is considered. The six-month window also allows incoming vacationers at the end of the year to experience it and contribute feedback.

Named owners

Specific individuals — myself included — accountable for setup, pack-down, and resolving any concerns. Not "everyone", which in practice tends to mean no one.

Open feedback channel

Anyone in the office can raise a concern at any time, directly to the named owners or anonymously through the committee.

In closing

A small bet on a stronger office.

If the trial succeeds, Canberra ends up with one of the more distinctive small-office cultures in the network for a one-off cost in the low hundreds. If it does not, very little has been lost. Either way, the people raising the suggestion have been heard.

I would welcome the opportunity to discuss this with each of you, and would be happy to set up a meeting at a time that suits — together or separately, whichever you prefer.
